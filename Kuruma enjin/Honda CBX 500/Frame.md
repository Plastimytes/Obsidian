
Building a Cafe racer frame for a Honda cb350 step by step and try to include possible measurements



Building a cafe racer frame for a Honda CB350 is a challenging but rewarding project. It involves modifying the existing frame or building a new one to achieve the desired cafe racer stance and aesthetics. Here's a step-by-step guide with possible measurements:

1. Planning and Design:

Research: Study various cafe racer designs and frame geometries. Pay attention to rake, trail, wheelbase, and seat height.
Sketch: Create detailed sketches of your desired frame design, considering the CB350 engine and other components.
Measurements: Take precise measurements of the CB350 engine, wheels, and other components that will be mounted on the frame.
Materials: Choose suitable materials for the frame, such as steel tubing (typically 1-1.5 inches diameter) or chromoly steel for a lighter and stronger frame.
1. Frame Modification or Construction:

Deconstruction: If modifying the existing frame, carefully remove unnecessary brackets and tabs.
Cutting and Shaping: Cut and shape the frame tubing according to your design, using a tube bender and other tools.
Welding: Weld the frame joints securely, ensuring proper alignment and strength. Consider using a jig to maintain accuracy.
Measurements: Continuously check measurements and alignment during the construction process.
1. Engine and Component Mounting:

Engine Mounts: Fabricate and weld engine mounts to the frame, ensuring proper alignment and clearance.
Other Mounts: Create mounts for the seat, fuel tank, handlebars, footpegs, and other components.
Measurements: Double-check all measurements and clearances to avoid issues later.
1. Suspension and Wheels:

Front Suspension: Choose a suitable front fork (often from a sportbike) and fabricate or modify triple clamps to fit the CB350's steering stem.
Rear Suspension: Decide on a rear suspension setup (typically dual shocks or a monoshock) and fabricate or modify mounts accordingly.
Wheels: Select appropriate wheels (often spoked wheels or lightweight aluminum rims) and ensure they fit the chosen suspension setup.
1. Seat and Bodywork:

Seat Pan: Fabricate a seat pan that fits the frame and provides a comfortable riding position.
Upholstery: Upholster the seat pan with foam and leather or vinyl.
Bodywork: Consider adding a custom-made fuel tank, front fairing, or rear cowl to enhance the cafe racer aesthetics.
1. Finishing Touches:

Painting or Powder Coating: Apply a protective and aesthetically pleasing finish to the frame.
Assembly: Assemble all components onto the frame, ensuring proper fit and function.
Testing: Test the motorcycle thoroughly to ensure safety and performance.
Possible Measurements:

Wheelbase: Aim for a shorter wheelbase than the stock CB350, typically around 54-56 inches.
Rake: Adjust the rake angle to achieve the desired steering characteristics, typically around 25-28 degrees.
Trail: Calculate and adjust the trail to ensure stable handling, typically around 3-4 inches.
Seat Height: Determine the desired seat height based on rider comfort and aesthetics, typically around 30-32 inches.
