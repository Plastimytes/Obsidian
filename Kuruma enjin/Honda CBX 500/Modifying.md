**Turbo**
**1. Planning and Research:**

- **Turbo Selection:** Choose a turbocharger appropriate for the CB350's engine size and desired power output. Smaller turbos are generally better for motorcycles due to space constraints and quicker spool. Research turbo maps to find one that matches your engine's characteristics.
- **Fuel Management:** The stock CB350 fueling system won't be sufficient for a turbocharged engine. You'll need to upgrade to a system that can deliver more fuel under boost. Options include:
    - **Carburetor Modification:** Modifying the stock carb or using a different carburetor designed for boost (like a Mikuni or Keihin) along with a boost-referenced fuel pressure regulator. This is the more "traditional" approach but requires very careful tuning.
    - **Electronic Fuel Injection (EFI):** Converting to EFI is the most modern and arguably best approach. It allows for precise fuel control and easier tuning. However, it's also the most complex and expensive option. You'll need a throttle body, fuel injectors, a fuel pump, and an ECU (Engine Control Unit) specifically designed for turbo applications.
- **Boost Control:** A boost controller allows you to adjust the amount of boost the turbocharger produces. This is crucial for managing power and preventing engine damage.
- **Intercooler (Recommended):** An intercooler cools the compressed air from the turbocharger before it enters the engine. This increases air density, leading to more power and reduced risk of detonation.
- **Exhaust System:** The stock exhaust system will likely be restrictive. You'll need a custom exhaust manifold to connect the turbocharger and a less restrictive exhaust system.
- **Engine Internals (Often Necessary):** The stock CB350 engine internals (pistons, connecting rods, crankshaft) are not designed for the stresses of forced induction. Depending on the boost level you plan to run, you'll likely need to upgrade these components to forged parts that can withstand the increased power. This requires a complete engine teardown.
- **Oil System:** The turbocharger needs a dedicated oil supply for lubrication. You'll need to tap into the engine's oil system and run lines to the turbocharger. A return line back to the engine's oil pan is also required.
- **Cooling System:** Turbocharging generates more heat. You might need to upgrade the cooling system with a larger radiator or an auxiliary oil cooler.

**2. Parts Acquisition:**

- Gather all the necessary components: turbocharger, fuel management system, boost controller, intercooler (if used), exhaust manifold, exhaust system, oil lines, gaskets, and any upgraded engine internals.

**3. Fabrication:**

- **Exhaust Manifold:** This is one of the most challenging parts. You'll need to fabricate a custom exhaust manifold to mount the turbocharger to the engine. This often requires welding skills and specialized equipment.
- **Intake Piping:** You'll need to fabricate or adapt intake piping to connect the turbocharger's compressor outlet to the throttle body (or carburetor).
- **Intercooler Mounting (If used):** If using an intercooler, you'll need to fabricate mounts and piping.
- **Oil Lines:** Fabricate or adapt oil lines for the turbocharger's lubrication.
- **Mounting Brackets:** You'll likely need to fabricate various brackets for mounting the turbocharger, fuel management components, and other parts.

**4. Installation:**

- **Engine Disassembly (Often Required):** If upgrading engine internals, you'll need to completely disassemble the engine.
- **Engine Reassembly:** Reassemble the engine with the upgraded internals.
- **Turbocharger Mounting:** Mount the turbocharger to the exhaust manifold.
- **Exhaust System Installation:** Install the custom exhaust system.
- **Fuel System Installation:** Install the upgraded fuel management system (carburetor or EFI).
- **Intake Piping Installation:** Install the intake piping.
- **Intercooler Installation (If used):** Install the intercooler and associated piping.
- **Oil Line Installation:** Install the oil lines for the turbocharger.
- **Cooling System Modifications:** Make any necessary modifications to the cooling system.
- **Wiring:** Connect all the necessary wiring for the fuel management system, boost controller, and other components.

**5. Tuning:**

- **Professional Tuning (Highly Recommended):** Tuning a turbocharged engine is critical. It's highly recommended to have the bike professionally tuned on a dyno. Improper tuning can lead to engine damage.
- **Fuel Mapping:** The fuel management system needs to be properly mapped to provide the correct air-fuel ratio at all boost levels.
- **Boost Control Adjustment:** Adjust the boost controller to achieve the desired boost level.

**6. Testing and Refinement:**

- **Test Rides:** After the initial tuning, perform test rides to ensure everything is working correctly.
- **Fine Tuning:** Make any necessary adjustments to the tuning and boost control.

**Very Important Considerations:**

- **Complexity:** This is a very complex project. Don't underestimate the skills and time required.
- **Cost:** Turbocharging a motorcycle can be expensive. Be prepared to invest a significant amount of money in parts and labor.
- **Reliability:** A turbocharged engine is more stressed than a naturally aspirated engine. Reliability can be a concern if the build is not done correctly.
- **Safety:** Improperly installed or tuned turbo systems can be dangerous and lead to engine damage or personal injury.

Again, this is a simplified overview. It's crucial to do extensive research, consult with experienced mechanics, and consider the complexity and cost before attempting this project. Professional help is highly recommended.


**Intake**
**1. Gather Your Tools and Supplies**

- **Air Intake Kit:** Choose a kit designed for the CB350 or one that's known to be compatible.
- **Tools:** Screwdrivers (Phillips and flathead), wrenches (various sizes), pliers, socket set, possibly a drill and drill bits.
- **Supplies:** New gaskets (if needed), threadlocker, cleaning supplies.

**2. Remove the Stock Airbox**

- **Disconnect Battery:** Disconnect the negative battery terminal for safety.
- **Remove Seat:** Take off the seat to access the airbox.
- **Detach Carburetors:** Loosen the clamps connecting the carburetors to the airbox and carefully remove the carburetors. You may need to disconnect fuel lines and other connections.
- **Remove Airbox:** Unbolt the airbox from the frame and remove it.

**3. Prepare the New Air Intake**

- **Assemble Intake:** Follow the instructions provided with your air intake kit to assemble the new intake components. This usually involves attaching the filter to a mounting bracket or adapter.
- **Clean Mating Surfaces:** Clean the carburetor inlets and the area where the intake will mount to ensure a good seal.

**4. Install the New Air Intake**

- **Mount Intake:** Attach the new air intake to the carburetors or intake manifolds, depending on the kit's design. Ensure a secure fit and tighten any clamps or bolts.
- **Connect Carburetors:** Reattach the carburetors to the new intake, making sure they are seated properly and the clamps are tightened.
- **Secure Intake:** If the kit includes mounting brackets, secure the intake to the frame.

**5. Reassemble and Test**

- **Reconnect Battery:** Reconnect the negative battery terminal.
- **Reinstall Seat:** Put the seat back on.
- **Start Engine:** Start the engine and let it idle. Listen for any unusual noises or leaks.
- **Test Ride:** Take the bike for a short test ride to ensure everything is working correctly.

**Important Considerations**

- **Tuning:** After installing an aftermarket air intake, you may need to adjust the carburetor settings to optimize performance and prevent running lean. This might involve changing jets or adjusting the air/fuel mixture screw.
- **Fitment:** Make sure the air intake kit is compatible with your CB350 model and year. Some kits may require modifications or adapters.
- **Instructions:** Always refer to the instructions provided with your specific air intake kit for detailed guidance and torque specifications.
- **Professional Help:** If you're not comfortable with motorcycle mechanics, it's best to seek help from a qualified professional.

**Additional Tips**

- **Cleanliness:** Keep everything clean during the installation process to prevent dirt or debris from entering the engine.
- **Gaskets:** Replace any old or damaged gaskets to ensure a good seal and prevent air leaks.
- **Threadlocker:** Use threadlocker on any bolts or screws to prevent them from coming loose due to vibrations.
